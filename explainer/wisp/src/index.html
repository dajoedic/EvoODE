The forward direction

A rule for right now

Imagine a population. Its size is called u. A differential equation tells us how quickly that size changes at this moment. To draw a curve, take a small step in time, update the size using the rule, and repeat.

The symbol on the left means “change in population per unit of time.” On the right, r controls growth and K is the capacity. Near capacity, growth slows. Move either slider to see a rule become a curve.

When things change together

Prey feed predators; predators eat prey. We need a rule for each population. The time plot shows when each rises and falls. The phase plane plots prey against predators: time is now the journey around the loop.

The inverse direction

Which pieces make the rule?

Now reverse the question. We have measurements, but we do not know the rule. Start with a catalogue of building blocks, called terms. A coefficient tells us how strongly each term acts. Switching on a term chooses a piece of the equation.

Structure means which terms are present. Parameters are their coefficients. A curve can look right even when its structure is wrong.

Trying every combination gets expensive quickly. Choose how many variables a system has. Each equation can use any subset of its catalogue; the total counts below include all equations and the empty subset.

A small disturbance, a big problem

Noise hides in the slope

A measurement is never perfect. Here each value is multiplied by a small random disturbance. The same disturbances are reused as you move the noise slider, so you see only the effect of their size.

A slope estimate subtracts neighbouring measurements and divides by their time gap. When the gap is small, a tiny measurement error becomes a large slope error. Compare the nearly smooth measurements with the jagged derivative estimate.

Replace a slope with a total

Add up the change

Instead of asking how fast the population changed at a single moment, ask how much it changed across a window. The left side is the last measurement minus the first. The right side adds up the rule over the same window. “Integral” is the mathematical name for that total.

Each shaded area below is a candidate term’s integral. Multiply the areas by coefficients and add them: the result should match the measured change. Integration accumulates nearby values instead of dividing their differences by a small time gap. It helps with noise; it does not make noise disappear.

A window becomes a row

Repeat this for overlapping windows of several lengths. Every row below is one window; every column is one candidate term. We now seek coefficients that make the weighted columns match the change column. This is linear regression: the terms may be curved, but their coefficients enter as a weighted sum.

A short list, one decision at a time

Grow. Refit. Clean up.

WISP starts with no terms. FROLS tries each unused term, refits all current coefficients, and chooses the largest reduction in remaining squared error. A large bar means useful extra information given what is already selected, rather than a good match in isolation.

FoBa then asks whether an older term has become redundant. It removes the cheapest term when its removal costs less than the permitted fraction of the latest forward gain. That lets an early guess leave when a better explanation arrives.

A removal recorded in Q5

The live example follows the frozen interim growth policy. Its cap and near-zero residual stop are displayed beside the path. This is an interim policy, not a claim that model size has been solved.

The evidence, with its limits

Does the equation hold up?

Data provenance

Recovering the exact terms and drawing the right curve are different achievements. Both stay visible below. Reconstruction starts from the clean training initial condition; generalization starts from the other initial condition. The curve metric asks how often the model explains enough variation to cross the displayed threshold.

Only exact systems enter these structure rates. Strata stay separate. Counts are cells; repeated initial conditions and noise draws do not create independent systems. No uncertainty interval or significance claim is inferred from the cell count.

Rates are rounded for display; their exported values and counts retain the aggregation’s precision.

A small glossary

Differential equation
A rule for the rate of change at the current state.
Trajectory
The curve produced by following a rule from a starting state.
Term / coefficient
A building block of a rule / the number multiplying that block.
Weak form
The equation integrated across windows, replacing local derivatives with accumulated change.
Residual
The difference between a measured target and the model’s prediction for it.
Structure
The set of terms present in the equation. Raw uses every stored term; pruned removes small coefficients using the frozen evaluation rule.
R²
A score comparing prediction error with variation around the data’s mean. A perfect prediction reaches unity; a score below zero is worse than predicting the mean.
Stratum / cell / system
A separate group of systems / a particular initial condition, noise and sampling draw / an underlying equation.
BIC
An information criterion trading fit against model size. Its role is explained in the next version.

Where the pieces come from

WISP combines established pieces. The weak form makes the search less sensitive to numerical derivatives; incremental growth keeps a short candidate list; backward removal lets early guesses leave; simulation and refitting test behaviour over time.

  • Sparse term catalogues: SINDy — Brunton, Proctor & Kutz.
  • Integral discovery: Schaeffer & McCalla; weak-form discovery: WSINDy — Messenger & Bortz.
  • Forward orthogonal least squares: FROLS — Billings, Chen & Korenberg.
  • Forward–backward greedy selection: FoBa — Tong Zhang.
  • Simulation and information criteria: Mangan, Kutz, Brunton & Proctor.

The ideas and worked systems follow the repository’s WISP cookbook. The frozen protocol and manifest identify the numerical evidence; illustrative controls are labelled separately.
